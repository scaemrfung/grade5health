19 Canada's Food Guide Plate (2019) 30 min · Half / quarter / quarter ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

Guiding question: To what extent does nutrition contribute to health and well-being? Use the 2019 plate, not the old rainbow. AHS Grade 5 Finding Nutritional Balance is a valid resource.

Materials

  • Week 19 worksheet
  • Optional Food Guide plate on projector

Warm-up (5 min)

Draw a circle. Cross out the old rainbow kindly. We use 2019 proportions at a meal.

Teach (8 min)

Read the plate

  1. 1Half: vegetables and fruits (fresh, frozen, or canned).
  2. 2One quarter: whole grain foods (oats, brown/wild rice, whole grain pasta or bread).
  3. 3One quarter: protein foods (beans, lentils, nuts, eggs, fish, lean meat, lower-fat dairy, fortified soy). Plant proteins more often.
  4. 4Water is the drink of choice.
  5. 5How we eat matters: meals together when we can, cook more often, no panic about one meal.
  6. 6Do not comment on anyone's lunch. Access is not equal.

Example story (read aloud)

Leila drew a circle and kindly crossed out the old rainbow she remembered from a poster in the hallway. Canada’s Food Guide in 2019 uses a plate: half vegetables and fruits, one quarter whole grain foods, one quarter protein foods. Water is the drink of choice.

Plate A was a meal she actually eats: whole-grain toast, scrambled egg, apple slices, and water. Not a chef fantasy. The toast was the grain quarter, egg the protein, apple the fruit. She marked partly because the plate was light on vegetables.

Plate B, one realistic change: add leftover carrots or frozen mixed vegetables on the side. Frozen and canned count. How we eat matters too — meals together when we can — but nobody commented on anyone’s lunch kit. Access is not equal. Why a plate of proportions instead of a rainbow of servings? Leila wrote: it is easier to picture at one meal, and it puts vegetables and fruits at half without counting cups in the cafeteria.

20 Nutrition, Hydration, and How the Body Uses Food 30 min · Energy and nourishment ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

Food is fuel, building, and repair. Hydration supports every system. Personal factors change needs.

Materials

  • Week 20 worksheet

Warm-up (5 min)

What does your body do with lunch besides 'not be hungry'? Collect: energy, growth, repair, thinking.

Teach (8 min)

Food is more than calories

  1. 1Carbohydrates fuel. Protein builds and repairs. Fats help absorb some vitamins. Fibre helps digestion. Vitamins/minerals are small but essential.
  2. 2Water carries nutrients, cools you, helps blood move. Thirst is a late signal.
  3. 3Needs differ: soccer night vs reading night; growth spurts; allergies; cultural fasting. Those are factors, not 'picky.'
  4. 4Food is not a moral test (good kid / bad kid). No calorie counting in Grade 5.

Example story (read aloud)

What does your body do with lunch besides not being hungry? Marcus listed energy, growth, repair, and thinking through math. Food is more than calories. Carbohydrates fuel. Protein builds and repairs. Fats help absorb some vitamins. Fibre helps digestion. Water carries nutrients and cools you. Thirst is a late signal.

He matched plate sections to jobs in his own words. Vegetables and fruits: vitamins, fibre, and colour that help the body work. Whole grains: longer-lasting energy. Protein: building and repair after gym. Water: helps blood move and keeps you from fading in the afternoon.

A personal factor that changes needs: soccer night versus a reading night, or an allergy in the class that means we do not share. Those are factors, not picky. High-activity day: extra water bottle and a protein-plus-fruit snack. Food is not a moral test. No calorie counting. Nutrition, Marcus wrote, contributes a lot to well-being — energy and mood — and it is not the only factor.

Student activity / worksheet (12 min)

Match plate sections to jobs; note one personal factor.

21 Supporting the Heart and Digestion 30 min · Cardiovascular and digestive systems ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

How nutrition and hydration support heart/blood and digestion. Patterns, not panic.

Materials

  • Week 21 worksheet

Warm-up (5 min)

Fist on chest (heart is a muscle). Trace mouth → stomach → intestines.

Teach (8 min)

Two systems, one lunch

  1. 1Digestive: breaks food down. Fibre + water help waste move. Eating slowly helps.
  2. 2Cardiovascular: heart and vessels move oxygen and nutrients. Everyday pattern of veg/fruit, whole grains, protein, water supports it. Sugary drinks and very salty snacks as the usual pattern make the system work harder.
  3. 3One birthday cake does not ruin a heart. Patterns matter.
  4. 4We do not diagnose from a Health lesson.

Example story (read aloud)

Anika made a fist on her chest. The heart is a muscle. She traced mouth to stomach to intestines with a finger in the air. Two systems, one lunch.

Digestive system: breaks food down. Fibre plus water help waste move. Eating slowly helps. Cardiovascular system: heart and vessels move oxygen and nutrients. A pattern of vegetables, fruit, whole grains, protein, and water supports it. Sugary drinks and very salty snacks as the usual after-school pattern make the system work harder.

One birthday cake does not ruin a heart. Patterns matter. They compared snacks. Apple plus a handful of nuts: fibre and protein, helpful over time. Large sugary drink plus chips most days: not a helpful usual pattern. Why does water show up on both lists? Anika wrote: it helps digestion move, and it helps blood carry what the heart is pumping. We do not diagnose from a Health lesson. We talk in often versus sometimes.

Student activity / worksheet (12 min)

Systems table + two-snack compare.

22 Supporting Muscles, Bones, and Reproductive Health 30 min · Musculoskeletal and reproductive systems ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

Nourishment for growing muscles, bones, and a maturing reproductive system. No pregnancy content. Puberty details are Weeks 25–30.

Materials

  • Week 22 worksheet

Warm-up (5 min)

Squeeze fists (muscles), tap a collarbone (bones). These systems are growing. Food is building supply.

Teach (8 min)

Building supplies

  1. 1Protein foods help muscles repair. Calcium-rich foods and vitamin D support bones. Water helps working muscles.
  2. 2The reproductive system is developing. The same habits that support overall growth — enough food, iron-rich foods, plate variety, water, sleep, activity — also support that development. We do not discuss pregnancy today.
  3. 3Iron: meat, beans, lentils, fortified cereal. Plant iron with fruit (vitamin C) helps absorption.
  4. 4Often skipping meals leaves a growing body short. Food scarcity is not the student's fault — school supports exist.

Example story (read aloud)

Connor squeezed his fists — muscles — and tapped a collarbone — bones. These systems are growing. Food is building supply. Protein foods help muscles repair after intramurals. Calcium-rich foods and vitamin D support bones: milk, fortified soy, yogurt. Water helps working muscles.

Iron: meat, beans, lentils, fortified cereal. Plant iron with fruit, which has vitamin C, helps absorption. The reproductive system is also developing, Mr. Fung said calmly. The same habits that support overall growth — enough food, iron-rich foods, plate variety, water, sleep, activity — support that development too. No pregnancy content. Puberty details wait for later weeks.

Why would often skipping meals make growing harder? Connor wrote: the body is short on building supplies, energy, and iron. He did not comment on anyone’s body size. Food scarcity is not a student’s fault. School supports exist. Nutrition supports well-being beyond not being hungry: bones, muscles, and having energy to learn.

Student activity / worksheet (12 min)

Connect plate sections to growing systems. No comments on anyone's size.

23 Cultural, Traditional, and Medicinal Food Practices 30 min · Including FNMI food knowledge ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

Food is culture, land, family, and sometimes medicine. First Nations, Métis, and Inuit food knowledge is diverse. No play-acting ceremony.

Materials

  • Week 23 worksheet

Warm-up (5 min)

Land acknowledgment. Add: today food is knowledge, not a costume. Optional: one family gathering food.

Teach (8 min)

Many ways to nourish

  1. 1Canada's Food Guide is a tool, not the only food knowledge.
  2. 2FNMI food systems are diverse and tied to land, water, season, and community — not one 'Indigenous menu.' Treaty 6 (EIPS): some communities include bison, deer, fish, berries, gardens, shared feasts.
  3. 3Some plants are food, some medicine, some ceremony. Ceremony is not a classroom craft unless a knowledge keeper leads it.
  4. 4Medicinal/comfort practices exist in many cultures (ginger tea, soup when sick). They do not replace a doctor.
  5. 5Respect: no 'weird food' faces. No student is a spokesperson for a whole people.

Example story (read aloud)

Before the lesson, the class heard the land acknowledgment. Mr. Fung added: today food is knowledge, not a costume. Elk Island schools are on Treaty 6 territory. We say some communities, never all.

Sage shared a family gathering food: bannock at her kokum’s table sometimes, with stew. Tomas shared Friday lentil soup at his house. Neither student was asked to be a spokesperson for a whole people. First Nations, Métis, and Inuit food systems are diverse and tied to land, water, season, and community — bison, deer, fish, berries, gardens, and shared feasts in some Treaty 6 communities, and many other practices.

Some plants are food, some medicine, some ceremony. Ceremony is not a classroom craft unless a knowledge keeper leads it. Comfort practices exist in many cultures — ginger tea, soup when sick — and they do not replace a doctor. A respectful response if someone brings a food you have never seen: That looks interesting. What is it called? No weird-food faces. Canada’s Food Guide is a tool, not the only food knowledge.

Student activity / worksheet (12 min)

One family or taught example; one Food Guide connection; one respectful table behaviour.

24 Finding Nutritional Balance — Assessing Meals 30 min · Unit wrap · AHS resource ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

Assess snacks and meals with the 2019 plate. Answer the guiding question. AHS Finding Nutritional Balance is valid support.

Materials

  • Week 24 worksheet
  • Optional AHS Balanced Bites items

Warm-up (5 min)

Rank closeness to the plate (not 'good kids'): cereal+milk+banana; pizza+salad+water; energy drink+candy.

Teach (8 min)

Assess, don't morally score

  1. 1Where is veg/fruit, protein, whole grain, water? What is the week's pattern?
  2. 2Balance often means adding, not only removing.
  3. 3Nutrition contributes a lot to energy, growth, mood, and body systems — and it is not the only factor (sleep, safety, relationships, activity).

Example story (read aloud)

Nina ranked three ideas for closeness to the plate, not for good kids: cereal with milk and a banana; pizza with salad and water; energy drink and candy. Meal 1 on the sheet was a whole grain wrap, chicken or beans, roasted carrots, apple, and water. She ticked every section present.

Carrots and apple support digestion and give fibre. The wrap is the grain quarter. Chicken or beans fill the protein quarter. Water helps every system. Meal 2: large sugary drink, instant noodles, no fruit or vegetables, most weekdays. Missing: vegetables and fruits, often protein, water as the drink. One realistic add: a banana or carrot sticks, and water most days, even if the noodles stay sometimes.

Guiding question: to what extent does nutrition contribute to health and well-being? Nina used the digestive system in her answer. Patterns of fibre, protein, and water help her body break down food and give steady energy for afternoon classes. Nutrition contributes a lot — and sleep, safety, relationships, and activity still matter. A non-food factor: enough sleep before a quiz.

Assess two meals; answer the guiding question. This page is unit evidence.

Healthy Eating — unit rubric (1–4)

Score once near the end of the six weeks. Use worksheets as evidence, not as 36 separate grades.

Categories (score each 1–4, then add):

  • 2019 plate language
  • Link to a body system
  • Respect for cultural / FNMI food knowledge
  • Realistic meal assessment

Proficient: average 3 or higher, and the student can explain one idea from the unit in their own words.

1 Beginning
Work is missing, copied, or does not show the idea yet.
2 Developing
Some of the idea is there, but examples are thin or mixed up.
3 Proficient
Clear, complete work that matches the outcome. A classmate could learn from it.
4 Extending
Goes further with a specific, honest example and a next step that fits real life.
