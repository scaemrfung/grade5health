25 Maturation and Changes in Adolescence 30 min · Physical, cognitive, behavioural ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students investigate maturation and identify changes during adolescence.

Adolescence is the stage between childhood and adulthood. Maturation is the process of growing and developing. Changes are physical, cognitive (thinking), and behavioural. Timing varies. No one is 'late' as a joke.

Materials

  • Week 25 worksheet
  • Question box

Warm-up (5 min)

Three columns: Body / Thinking / Behaviour. Students add one change they have noticed in general (not pointing at a classmate).

Teach (8 min)

Adolescence is a range, not a race

  1. 1Physical: growth spurts, sleep need, appetite, sweating, skin, body shape, hair in new places. These connect to hormones — chemical messengers.
  2. 2Cognitive: longer-term thinking starts to grow; you can still feel very in-the-moment.
  3. 3Behavioural: want more independence, care more what peers think, mood can swing. That is common, not a character flaw.
  4. 4Everyone's timeline is different. Comparing bodies in class is not allowed.
  5. 5Questions go in the box. We answer facts next class. We do not answer personal questions about anyone in the room.

Example story (read aloud)

Ibrahim did not point at anyone. He added to the columns on the board: Body — needing more sleep; Thinking — caring more about a group project going well; Behaviour — wanting a closed door when doing homework. Adolescence, Mr. Fung said, is the stage between childhood and adulthood. Maturation is the process of growing and developing. Timing varies. No one is late as a joke.

Physical changes connect to hormones, which are chemical messengers. Cognitive changes: longer-term thinking starts to grow, and you can still feel very in-the-moment. Behavioural: more independence, more attention to peers, mood can swing. That is common, not a character flaw.

Why is it unkind to rank who hit puberty first? Ibrahim wrote: bodies are not a race, and ranking makes people feel watched. Comparing bodies in class is not allowed. Questions go in the box. We answer facts next class. We do not answer personal questions about anyone in the room. Giggles happened. Mr. Fung reset once — calm science voices — and continued. Nobody was shamed for giggling.

26 Physical Changes During Puberty 30 min · Hormones and secondary characteristics ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students investigate maturation and identify changes during adolescence. Students connect puberty to the capacity for human reproduction.

Puberty is the set of physical changes that lead toward adult reproductive capacity. Hormones increase. Secondary sex characteristics appear. Timing varies.

Materials

  • Week 26 worksheet

Warm-up (5 min)

Define hormone in one kid sentence: a chemical message that tells the body to change or do a job.

Teach (8 min)

What puberty does

  1. 1Pituitary gland in the brain signals ovaries or testicles to make more hormones (estrogen, progesterone, testosterone — names on the board, not a memorization test).
  2. 2Primary changes: reproductive organs mature (next two weeks).
  3. 3Secondary characteristics: breast development; voice deepening; hair under arms, on legs, on the face, and in the pubic area; wider hips or wider shoulders; more oil on skin; more sweat; growth spurt.
  4. 4People assigned female, male, or who develop differently may notice different mixes and timings. Stick to facts. No commenting on a classmate's body.
  5. 5Voice cracks, extra laundry, and needing deodorant are normal. Hygiene is respect for yourself and others.

Example story (read aloud)

Hormone, Sophie said in one kid sentence: a chemical message that tells the body to change or do a job. The pituitary gland in the brain signals ovaries or testicles to make more hormones. Names went on the board: estrogen, progesterone, testosterone — not a memorization test.

The class sorted fact and myth. Everyone starts puberty at the same age: myth. Hormones are chemical messengers: fact. Sweat and skin oil often increase: fact. You can tell everything about a person from how fast they grow: myth.

Secondary characteristics were listed as science, not as a checklist of classmates: breast development, voice deepening, hair in new places, wider hips or wider shoulders, a growth spurt. People may notice different mixes and timings. Voice cracks, extra laundry, and needing deodorant are normal. Hygiene is respect. Sophie wrote two general physical changes a person might notice — not naming a classmate. Her trusted adult for a puberty question: Mr. Fung or a parent. No commenting on a classmate’s body.

27 Puberty and Reproductive Capacity 30 min · Organs and functions ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students connect puberty to the capacity for human reproduction.

Each part of the human reproductive system has a job. Puberty matures those parts so the body becomes capable of reproduction in the future. We name parts and functions. We do not teach fertilization through birth (Grade 6).

Materials

  • Week 27 worksheet
  • Simple unlabeled diagrams if your school provides them — or word lists only

Warm-up (5 min)

Rules: correct words, calm voice, no jokes about anyone in the room. Pass is allowed.

Teach (8 min)

Parts and jobs (Grade 5)

  1. 1Female system (typical): ovaries store egg cells; uterus is a muscular organ with a lining; vagina is a canal from the uterus to the outside; vulva is the outside parts. Fallopian tubes carry an egg toward the uterus — name them, do not teach fertilization/implantation stories.
  2. 2Male system (typical): testicles produce sperm cells and testosterone; scrotum holds the testicles; penis delivers urine and, later, semen. Sperm production is a sign of reproductive capacity (Week 28).
  3. 3These systems connect to other systems (hormones, blood). Growing them is part of overall maturation.
  4. 4Having these organs mature means the body is gaining the capacity for human reproduction. Capacity is not the same as being ready to parent, and it is not a request to discuss sexual activity.
  5. 5Drawings stay scientific. No phones out.

Example story (read aloud)

Rules on the board: correct words, calm voice, no jokes about anyone in the room. Pass is allowed. Ravi matched organs to jobs in Grade 5 language.

Ovaries store egg cells. The uterus is a muscular organ with a lining. The vagina is a canal from the uterus to the outside; the vulva is the outside parts. Testicles produce sperm cells and testosterone. The scrotum holds the testicles. The penis delivers urine and, later, semen.

Having these organs mature means the body is gaining the capacity for human reproduction. Capacity is not the same as being ready to parent, and it is not a request to discuss sexual activity. They did not teach fertilization, implantation, or birth — that is Grade 6. Why correct anatomical words instead of slang? Ravi wrote: slang turns the lesson into a joke contest and can humiliate people. Correct words keep it science. Drawings stayed scientific. No phones out.

28 Menstruation and Sperm Production 30 min · Signs of reproductive capacity ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students connect puberty to the capacity for human reproduction.

Menstruation and sperm production are processes that appear during puberty. They signal reproductive capacity. Factual, respectful, all students learn both. No fertilization-to-birth.

Materials

  • Week 28 worksheet
  • Optional: unopened pad/tampon as objects to demystify, not a how-to-have-sex prop

Warm-up (5 min)

Everyone learns everyone else's basic biology. You may need to support a friend or sibling someday.

Teach (8 min)

Two processes, one idea: capacity

  1. 1Menstruation: about once a month, if an egg is not going to start a pregnancy, the uterus lining leaves the body through the vagina as blood and tissue. This is a period. It can start during puberty. Pads, period underwear, or tampons (with adult guidance) are ways people manage it. Cramps and mood changes can happen. Tell an adult if bleeding is very heavy or very painful.
  2. 2We do not teach the fertilization-and-pregnancy sequence in Grade 5. We only need: periods are tied to a cycle that is part of reproductive capacity.
  3. 3Sperm production: during puberty, testicles begin to make sperm cells. Semen is the fluid that can carry sperm. Nocturnal emissions ('wet dreams') can happen and are not something a person chooses. They are a sign the system is maturing.
  4. 4Hygiene: change pads, wash, clean clothes. All bodies deserve privacy in change rooms.
  5. 5If you get your period at school: go to the office or a trusted teacher. We have supplies. You will not be in trouble.

Example story (read aloud)

Everyone learned both processes. Grace asked a question from the box: If this is about capacity, why do we all need to know? Because you may need to support a friend or sibling someday, and because it is ordinary biology.

Menstruation: about once a month, if an egg is not going to start a pregnancy, the uterus lining leaves the body through the vagina as blood and tissue. That is a period. Pads, period underwear, or tampons with adult guidance are ways people manage it. If a period starts at school: go to the office or a trusted teacher. Supplies exist. You will not be in trouble.

Sperm production can begin during puberty. Semen is the fluid that can carry sperm. Nocturnal emissions can happen and are not something a person chooses. They are a sign the system is maturing. Wet dreams do not mean someone did something wrong. Only girls need to learn about periods: false. We are learning the full pregnancy story this week: false. A kind, private response if a classmate is upset: walk them to supplies, do not announce it.

Student activity / worksheet (12 min)

29 Positive Health Practices During Puberty 30 min · Sleep, food, hygiene, activity, adults ▼
5 warm-up 8 teach 12 worksheet 5 close

Alberta PEW Grade 5 outcome

Students investigate maturation and identify changes during adolescence. Identify positive health practices during puberty.

Physical activity, nutritious food, sleep, hygiene, stress reduction, and trusted adults support well-being while the body changes.

Materials

  • Week 29 worksheet

Warm-up (5 min)

List on the board: sleep, food, movement, wash, talk. Students rank which is hardest for them this month (private on the sheet, not out loud).

Teach (8 min)

Practices that support a changing body

  1. 1Sleep: growing bodies often need more. Phones out of the bed if the family can.
  2. 2Food: enough and varied (link back to the plate). Appetite can jump. That can be normal.
  3. 3Movement: activity supports mood, bones, and heart.
  4. 4Hygiene: daily wash, deodorant if needed, clean clothes, period care.
  5. 5Stress: name it, move, talk, rest. Huge mood swings that last and get in the way of life — tell an adult.
  6. 6Trusted adults: same list from Week 15. Puberty questions are allowed.

Example story (read aloud)

Grace ranked privately, not out loud, which practice felt hardest this month: sleep. Growing bodies often need more. Phones out of the bed if the family can. Food: enough and varied, using plate language from the eating unit. Appetite can jump. That can be normal. Movement supports mood, bones, and heart. Hygiene: daily wash, deodorant if needed, clean clothes, period care — handled as facts, not a public shaming.

Stress: name it, move, talk, rest. Huge mood swings that last and get in the way of life — tell an adult. She built a realistic week, not a perfect one. Sleep: bag the tablet in the kitchen at 8:30 two school nights. Nutrition and water: water bottle at her desk. Activity: intramurals Tuesday. Hygiene: deodorant after gym. Talking to a trusted adult: ask Mr. Fung one box question on Thursday.

The practice that would help most in seven days: sleep, because afternoon science feels foggy. Trusted adults still counted. Nobody’s laundry was discussed in front of the class.
