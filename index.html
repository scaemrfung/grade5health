How to use this site

Open the unit page for this week. Expand the lesson. Teach from the numbered steps. Photocopy or print the worksheet (12 minutes). Close with the check for understanding. That is the whole block.

Everything a sub needs is on the unit page: outcome, materials, steps, activity, worksheet, check, and a teacher tip. No extra binder required.

How the weekly block works

Keep the clock visible. Do not stretch the teach. The worksheet is the learning, not an extra.

5 min

Warm-up. One question or short scenario. Students talk, then you collect two answers.

8 min

Teach. Short, numbered steps. Board notes stay simple. Students listen and watch a model.

12 min

Activity / worksheet. Independent or pairs. Short enough to finish. Collect or stamp.

5 min

Close. Check for understanding. One sentence out the door. Preview next week in one line.

Classroom norms for Health. Speak about yourself, not other people’s bodies or money. No names when sharing hard stories. Pass is always allowed. What classmates share stays in the room unless safety is involved — then tell Mr. Fung or the office.

Outcomes map

Character Development · Weeks 1–6

Organizing idea: Exploration of life opportunities and virtues develops resilience and personal talents and promotes lifelong learning.

Learning outcome: Students reflect and relate life experiences to perseverance and well-being.

Skills you will see: examine how experiences shape understanding; connect knowledge and skills to volunteering; identify protective factors; describe effects of perseverance on well-being.

Safety · Weeks 13–18

Organizing idea: A lifetime of optimal well-being is supported by prioritizing health and safety.

Learning outcome: Students analyze responsibility and consider the impact on well-being.

Skills you will see: consequences of actions; laws, rules, practices, protocols; personal and group safety; digital privacy; Grade 5-appropriate consent and refusal.

Healthy Eating · Weeks 19–24

Organizing idea: A lifetime of optimal well-being and physical wellness is supported by prioritizing nutrition and healthy eating.

Learning outcome: Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

Guiding question: To what extent does nutrition contribute to health and well-being?

Canada’s Food Guide 2019 plate (not the old rainbow). Nutrition and hydration effects on cardiovascular, digestive, musculoskeletal, and reproductive systems. Cultural, traditional, and medicinal food practices, including First Nations, Métis, and Inuit. Valid resource: AHS Grade 5 Finding Nutritional Balance.

Growth & Development · Weeks 25–30

Learning outcomes: Students investigate maturation and identify changes during adolescence. Students connect puberty to the capacity for human reproduction.

Age-appropriate, factual, respectful. Puberty changes, reproductive organs and functions, menstruation and sperm production, positive health practices. No Grade 6 fertilization-to-birth content. Send the family letter before this unit.

How we score (one rubric per unit)

Do not score every worksheet out of 4. Use worksheets as formative. Use the unit rubric once near the end of the six weeks, plus a quick process check.

Scale for every unit: 1 Beginning · 2 Developing · 3 Proficient · 4 Extending. Proficient is the target. Full descriptors live on each unit page.

  • Character: Reflection · Connection to perseverance · Community contribution · Exit thinking
  • Relationships: Perspective-taking · Listening · Respectful talk · Shared understanding
  • Safety: Responsibility · Safety choices · Digital privacy · Consent/refusal
  • Healthy eating: Food Guide plate · Body systems · Cultural food respect · Meal assessment
  • Growth: Accurate language · Maturation changes · Health practices · Respectful participation
  • Money: Needs/wants · Goals & saving · Budget · Consumer thinking

Teacher notes for Growth & Development

Do this before Week 25. Send the family letter at least one month before instruction. Alberta’s Education Act requires parent/guardian notice and the option to opt out of human sexuality instruction. Keep returned slips in a folder. Students who opt out complete an alternate wellness task in a supervised space — never a punishment, never a public list.

Privacy in the room

  • No personal questions about anyone’s body, family, or development timeline.
  • Anonymous question box. Answer factually next class. Skip anything that is not Grade 5 PEW.
  • Students may pass. They may write instead of speaking.
  • Use correct anatomical words (penis, testicles, vulva, vagina, uterus, ovaries) calmly. Giggles are normal. Reset once, then continue.
  • Do not share student comments with the class as “an example.”

What we teach — and what we do not

Teach: maturation; physical, cognitive, and behavioural changes; puberty and hormones; secondary sex characteristics; reproductive organs and their functions; menstruation and sperm production as signs of reproductive capacity; positive health practices (sleep, nutrition, activity, hygiene, trusted adults).

Do not teach in Grade 5: fertilization through pregnancy and birth (that is Grade 6). Sexual activity, contraception, and dating advice. Gender identity lessons are not part of this unit plan — stay on the PEW growth outcomes named above.

If a student discloses a safety concern

Stop, thank them for telling you, and follow EIPS reporting procedures. Do not promise secrecy. Do not investigate. Inform the principal and complete the required report.
