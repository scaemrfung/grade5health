Teacher only · Growth & Development

Alternate wellness task (Weeks 25–30)

For: students who do not have a signed opt-in for Growth & Development (Weeks 25–30). Time: six 30-minute sessions, one per week, in a supervised space arranged with your principal.

Outcome (non-sensitive): Physical Education and Wellness, Grade 5. Character Development. Learning Outcome: “Students reflect and relate life experiences to perseverance and well-being.”

Never announce who is on this task, and never treat it as a punishment. Keep the list private. Check EIPS procedure with your principal for supervision and for how to report Growth & Development for these students (for example, N/A or as the school directs). This task gives Character Development evidence, not Growth & Development evidence.

Plan for the six sessions

  1. Session 1 · Choose a hard thing. Pick one skill or habit you can practise for six weeks at school, for example a juggling pattern, a skipping count, a drawing skill, a reading goal, or a kindness habit. Write what it is, why it matters to you, and your plan for each session.
  2. Session 2 · An experience that shaped me. Think of an experience that changed how you handle hard things. It can be small and does not need private details. Write: what happened, what you learned, and how it changed what you do now.
  3. Session 3 · My protective factors. List the people, places, habits, and tools that help you keep going. Make a protective-factor card with at least one item in three categories (people, habits, places or tools).
  4. Session 4 · Check and change. Practise, then log your progress. What is working? What is not? Choose one new strategy: rest and come back, try a new plan, or ask for help.
  5. Session 5 · Perseverance and well-being. Practise again. Then write how working at your hard thing has affected your mood, confidence, health, or sense of belonging.
  6. Session 6 · Teach a younger student. Make a one-page poster or letter for a younger student called “How to keep going on a hard thing”. End with this sentence: “Because of ____, I practised perseverance by ____, and that helped my well-being because ____.”

Materials

  • The printed student sheet below (2 pages).
  • Any simple practice materials for the chosen skill (for example, beanbags, a skipping rope, or a sketchbook), approved by the supervising adult.

Assessment

  • Use the Character Development rubric rows “Reflection” and “Connection to perseverance” on the unit page.
  • Solidifying Expectations looks like: names a real experience, describes concrete actions (including rest or a new plan), and names a protective factor linked to well-being.
  • Evidence: the student sheet (product) and a 2-minute talk in Session 6 (conversation).

For the supervising adult

  • Take attendance and keep the routine the same each week.
  • Collect the sheet at the end of each session and leave a short written note for the teacher.
  • Follow ISP supports listed in the class binder.
