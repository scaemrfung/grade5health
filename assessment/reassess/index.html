Second-chance (reassessment) tasks

One short task per unit, tied to the same outcome as the unit. Offer it to any student who wants to show a different level, especially students at Not Yet Meeting or Minimally Meeting Expectations, or with INS. It takes about 2 minutes as a conversation or 10 minutes as a redo sheet.

Catch-up weeks on this year’s school calendar (from the site’s school-year settings): Dec 14–18, 2026; Feb 1–3, 2027; June 21–25, 2027. No new lesson is planned in these weeks, so they suit second chances.

Record the new level on the class checklist. Use the most recent, most consistent evidence, and enter the final level in PowerTeacher Pro. Keep conversations private and calm. Never ask for personal or family details.

Character Development · Weeks 1–6

Character Development · second-chance task

Same outcome: Students reflect and relate life experiences to perseverance and well-being.

Type: 2-minute conversation · Suggested time: Dec 14–18 catch-up week, before the January report card.

Ask

  1. “Tell me about one time something was hard and you kept going. What did you actually do?”
  2. “What or who helped you keep going?” (a protective factor)
  3. “How did keeping going help your well-being: your mood, confidence, health, or belonging?”

Redo option: rewrite the Week 6 exit sentence with a new example. “Because of ____, I practised perseverance by ____, and that helped my well-being because ____.”

Look-fors (Solidifying Expectations)

  • Names a real experience (it can be small).
  • Describes a concrete action, not only “don’t give up”.
  • Names a protective factor and links it to well-being.

Healthy Relationships · Weeks 7–12

Healthy Relationships · second-chance task

Same outcome: Students acknowledge and connect perspectives of self and others through communication and listening skills.

Type: 2-minute conversation · Suggested time: Dec 14–18 catch-up week, before the January report card.

Ask

  1. Read aloud: “Two classmates both want the last library computer. Amir has a project due tomorrow. Jess waited 10 minutes in line.”
  2. “Say back what each person needs, in your own words.” (paraphrase)
  3. “Ask me one curious question about the situation.”
  4. “What next step could both people live with?”

Redo option: a short sheet with the same scenario. Write each person’s view fairly, one curious question, and a shared next step.

Look-fors (Solidifying Expectations)

  • States both views fairly, without picking a winner.
  • Paraphrases accurately and asks a curious (not trap) question.
  • Names overlap and a realistic next step.

Safety · Weeks 13–18

Safety · second-chance task

Same outcome: Students analyze responsibility and consider the impact on well-being.

Type: 2-minute conversation · Suggested time: Within Weeks 17–18 (January) if needed for the January report card; otherwise the Feb 1–3 catch-up week.

Ask

  1. Read aloud: “A friend sends you a funny photo of a classmate and says, ‘Forward it to the group.’”
  2. “Is this share, ask first, or never? Why?”
  3. “Who is affected, and how could it affect their well-being?”
  4. “What is the responsible next step if it was already forwarded?”

Redo option: the same scenario on paper, plus one consent scenario from Week 18 (space, games, borrowing, or digital sharing). Grade 5 contexts only. No personal stories.

Look-fors (Solidifying Expectations)

  • Sorts the choice with a reason.
  • Names an impact on someone else, not only “I’d get in trouble”.
  • Names a repair or responsible next step, including telling an adult when needed.

Healthy Eating · Weeks 19–24

Healthy Eating · second-chance task

Same outcome: Students evaluate aspects of nutrition and examine their benefits to well-being.

Type: short redo sheet · Suggested time: Any class after Week 24, or the June 21–25 catch-up week.

Ask

  1. Sample meal (not a student’s own lunch): a cheese sandwich on white bread, a juice box, and two cookies.
  2. “Use the Canada’s Food Guide plate. What is there, and what is missing?”
  3. “Name one realistic change a Grade 5 student could ask for or make.”
  4. “To what extent does this meal support well-being? Give a claim, an example, and one limit.”

Conversation option: ask the same questions out loud and record the answer on the class checklist.

Look-fors (Solidifying Expectations)

  • Uses half / quarter / quarter and water.
  • Names one realistic change.
  • Answers “to what extent” with a claim, an example, and a limit, without shaming any food or family.

Growth & Development · Weeks 25–30

Growth & Development · second-chance task

Same outcome: Students investigate maturation and identify changes during adolescence.
Students connect puberty to the capacity for human reproduction.

Type: 2-minute conversation (opted-in students only) · Suggested time: Within Weeks 28–30, or the June 21–25 catch-up week.

Ask

  1. “Name one physical, one thinking, and one behaviour change that can happen in adolescence.”
  2. “Name two positive health practices during puberty and how they help.”
  3. “What does ‘capacity for reproduction’ mean, and what does it not mean?”

Redo option: the student redoes the Week 27 “Organs and jobs” sheet from the locked Growth unit packet. Use correct anatomical words. Pass is allowed, and a written answer is fine.

Look-fors (Solidifying Expectations)

  • Uses correct terms calmly.
  • Identifies changes in more than one category and notes that timing varies.
  • Explains capacity vs ready to parent. No personal questions about anyone’s body.

Financial Literacy · Weeks 31–36

Financial Literacy · second-chance task

Same outcome: Students demonstrate how planning can support financial goals.

Type: short redo sheet · Suggested time: The June 21–25 catch-up week.

Ask

  1. Sample: “You get $15 a month. You want a $24 board game.”
  2. “Write a goal with an amount and a number of weeks, and a weekly save.”
  3. “Make a tiny budget that balances: money in = needs + wants + save + donate.”
  4. “Name one influence you will notice before you buy.”

Conversation option: talk through the same plan and record the answer on the class checklist. Sample dollars only; never family income.

Look-fors (Solidifying Expectations)

  • Goal has a number and a time.
  • Budget balances, with saving as a planned line.
  • Names an influence and connects the choice to the plan.
